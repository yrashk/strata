The reference emulator, compiled to WebAssembly

Run Strata in your browser

This is emu/emu.c, the same C emulator as on the desktop, with a JIT that turns hot Strata code into WebAssembly on the fly. Pick something to run. Click the terminal (or the screen) and type; Ctrl-C is the break key, just like at a real terminal.

What is running

  • The emulator runs in a Web Worker, continuously; this page only paints the screen and passes your keys along. The timer is real time, so a 10 ms preemption tick in StrataOS is 10 ms of your wall clock, and the screen's VSYNC is 60 Hz.
  • With JIT on (the default; ?jit=0 turns it off), hot Strata code is translated to WebAssembly while it runs: loops and the functions they call become one WebAssembly function, compiled by your browser and called through the emulator's function table. Expect a few thousand MIPS (millions of Strata instructions per second); the status line shows the rate.
  • With JIT off you get the reference interpreter with its exact one-instruction-per-core interleaving (a predecoded fast loop keeps it at hundreds of MIPS on plain code).
  • The disk is a copy in memory: whatever StrataOS writes is gone after Reset.
  • On the desktop: make boot, make show-gfx, make forth.
